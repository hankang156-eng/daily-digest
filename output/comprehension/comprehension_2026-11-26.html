AI-assisted proofs threaten cryptographic hardness assumptions

2 items · 2 new today · tracked since 2026-11-26

New thread: Google's Cogentic papers and factoring-in-P rumors spook cryptographers

This is a newly opened thread: Google released 'Cogentic' papers claiming new AI-driven results on integer factorization and discrete logarithms, landing the same week unverified rumors circulated that some lab may have found a polynomial-time factoring algorithm — which would break RSA.

Why it mattersRSA and much of public-key cryptography rely on the assumption that factoring large numbers is computationally hard (not solvable in polynomial time, i.e. 'not in P'). If that assumption falls, it doesn't just affect theoretical computer science — it undermines the security basis of HTTPS, banking, and countless legacy embedded systems that are hard to patch. Nothing here is confirmed yet; the next real move is independent verification of Cogentic's claims or a credible disclosure of an actual P-time factoring algorithm, as opposed to rumor.
