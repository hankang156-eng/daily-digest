### AI infrastructure

#### Data-center buildout meets grid and community friction
*42 items · 1 new today · tracked since 2026-06-20*

**Profit-sharing enters the political ask, beyond siting fights**

The friction story has mostly been about zoning rejections, pollution complaints, and coal-plant extensions. Today's NYT piece marks a shift toward a more structural demand: policymakers pushing to force data-center operators to share profits with host communities through taxes and public benefits, not just mitigate local harms.

**Why it matters:** This moves the fight from 'can you build here' to 'what do you owe us for building here' — a durable revenue claim rather than a one-time siting concession. If this framing spreads, expect tax and benefit-sharing terms to become as standard a negotiating line as community benefit agreements already are, adding another cost and timeline variable to hyperscaler buildout plans.

- [As A.I. Data Centers Spread, Pressure Mounts to Share Profits](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/16/business/ai-data-centers.html) — NYT

#### Hyperscalers and DOE chase new capacity to feed AI power demand
*27 items · 1 new today · tracked since 2026-06-24*

**IPPs move from vendor to strategic partner in power sourcing**

Recent items have focused on new generation capacity (geothermal, nuclear, clean energy targets) and grid-side software fixes. Today's piece reframes Independent Power Producers specifically as central players data-center developers now court directly to secure on-site generation, rather than treating power as a commodity purchased from the grid.

**Why it matters:** IPPs build and operate power plants independently of utilities, so partnering directly with them lets developers bypass slow utility interconnection queues and lock in dedicated generation — often gas, but increasingly the same nuclear/geothermal/clean sources this thread has tracked. Watch for hyperscalers announcing direct IPP deals or equity stakes as the next concrete step, since that would signal power sourcing is becoming as strategically owned as chip supply.
